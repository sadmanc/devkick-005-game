# How to win (time ignored, judges only)

Sadman asked: don't think about time, just what the judges will like. Ranked by how much each move is
worth. Built from event.md, judges.md, chroma-awards.md and tools.md.

## 1. It has to run, live, in the judges' hands
devkick's whole culture is "demo or it didn't happen". The best possible demo is a judge playing it on
their own phone within 10 seconds of a QR code, and laughing or yelling. Zero loading screens, zero
crashes, zero "imagine if". A smaller game that works beats a bigger one that almost works.

## 2. Make the AI part of the fun, not just part of the build
The jam is run by AI people (Cognition, UTMIST, ElevenLabs, Meshy). A game where the AI is a character
you play against or with gets remembered. Best version: a **live voice** (ElevenLabs) that reacts to the
exact thing you just did, by name, in under a second or two. This is what Yohann put in his own game.
But keep it as seasoning on a real game: the Chroma judges say "AI is not why a project wins".

## 3. Every sponsor tool does a visible job
- Devin: all the code, through PRs, reviewed, tested, several sessions at once.
- ElevenLabs: voices + music + SFX, with voice as a mechanic.
- Meshy: the hero character and key props, rigged and animated.
- Modal: the live voice/AI backend and a shared leaderboard or rooms.
One game that uses all four significantly qualifies for the category prize, Meshy Game of the Year,
Meshy Awards and ElevenLabs Awards at once.

## 4. Pick the Chroma category on purpose, and hit its words
Recommended: **Arcade & Party**. Its brief ("enjoy within seconds", "social", "quick rounds",
"friendly competition", "game feel") is exactly what wins a live demo room. Judges can all play at once.
Runner-up: **Music & Rhythm**, if the idea is built around sound; it is ElevenLabs' home turf and likely
has fewer entries. Say the category's own words in the pitch.

## 5. Craft and feel (what the Chroma judges score)
Game feel, mechanics, art direction, audio, polish. This is Sadman's edge from Roblox: use the
`nintendo-feel` and `reactive-feel` skills' rules. Instant press feedback, hit-stop, screen-free
satisfying sounds on every action, a big reward moment, a clear first 10 seconds without a tutorial.
One consistent art direction (one Meshy style reference for everything).

## 6. Tell the build story with real numbers
Cognition wants something it can post. End the demo with one slide: "2.5 hours, N Devin sessions in
parallel, N PRs, N reviewed by Devin Review, N tests, N Meshy models, N ElevenLabs voice lines, N live
lines generated during this demo." Copy Downhill's habit of stating numbers. Sadman's honest line:
"I can't code; I directed Devin." That is Cognition's dream testimonial.

## 7. Ship the full Chroma entry, not just a jam demo
Public link (Cloudflare Pages), gameplay video, tools tagged with proof, English text, original assets.
Being semifinal-ready on the night shows seriousness. Then also enter the separate
**Behind-the-Scenes: Game Division** with a 1-10 minute making-of video (screen recordings of Devin
sessions are perfect for it).

## 8. Be the team people remember
Show up to the networking hour, talk to Yohann and Jenny, post on X/LinkedIn tagging Chroma, Devin,
ElevenLabs and Meshy (Chroma's "Submission of the Day"). devkick invites people who ship to "The Table".

## Things that would lose
- A Roblox game (not web; doesn't meet Chroma rules).
- Copying an existing game or its characters (Chroma: original work only).
- Generic "AI-generated" look with no clear art direction.
- A demo that needs explaining before anyone can play.
- Using a sponsor tool only for something nobody can see.
- Anything that could read as insensitive (Chroma's zero-tolerance line).
