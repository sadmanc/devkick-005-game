# Seed material: Sound Local launch (for audience simulation)

Use this file as the seed for an audience simulation (run by hand in a Devin session, or uploaded to
MiroFish). It describes the product and three launch variants to test.

## The product
Sound Local is a voice game. You travel a world map and learn real English accents and slang by talking out
loud to local characters who judge you by ear. Each region: a 2-minute "Prove You're Local" placement test,
5-8 short fundamentals lessons (tonality: melody, rhythm, key sounds; grammar and slang patterns), then quests
(the core): goal-driven conversations like ordering at a Glasgow chippy from Senga the seagull or getting on
Big Hamish's bus. Characters answer in AI local voices, a trust meter moves, you get tips and a pitch line
of your voice vs. the local's. Open free talk is a Pro feature.

Built: Glasgow, Scotland (three characters, 3D street). Web game, built in one day at the Devin game jam.

## Pricing being tested
- Free: placement test, fundamentals, 1 quest per day, 1 free-talk conversation per day.
- Pro: $9.99/month or $59.99/year (all regions, unlimited quests, fair-use free talk, detailed feedback).
- Weekly pass: $2.99.
- Actor tier: $24.99/month (script upload, line-by-line coaching, dialect breakdown export).

## Launch variants
- **Variant A (respect framing):** "Can you convince Senga you're from Glasgow? Learn real accents from the
  locals who speak them. Next: Indian English, made with Indian creators, and Singlish."
- **Variant B (stereotype framing):** "Do the funniest accents in the world! Master the Indian and Chinese
  accent. Perfect party trick."
- **Variant C (performer framing):** "Your dialect coach in your pocket. Prep any accent for your next role."

## Questions for the simulation
1. Which segments react, share, sign up or attack, per variant?
2. What do Indian, Singaporean, Chinese-diaspora and Glaswegian audiences say?
3. What price objections come up? Which tier is each segment drawn to?
4. Do players want quests or lessons? What makes them come back?
5. What risks or backlash spread, and what stops them?
