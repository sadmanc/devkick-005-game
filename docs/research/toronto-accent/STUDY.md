## 1. Two Torontos, one city

Toronto English is really two overlapping things, and the unit teaches both:

1. **General Toronto / Canadian English**: the standard English of English-speaking Canada, which Toronto
   shares with most of the country (Labov, Ash & Boberg, *Atlas of North American English*, ch. 15; Chambers 2016).
   It is close to General American, so tourists mostly notice a few vowels, a few words and a few habits.
2. **Multicultural Toronto English (MTE)**: a youth register, or "feature pool", with roots in the Jamaican,
   Somali, Arabic, South Asian, West African and Filipino communities of the inner suburbs: Jane-Finch, Rexdale,
   Malvern and Scarborough (Denis 2016; Denis et al. 2023; Douglas & Liang 2024). It was spread by Toronto hip-hop,
   from Michie Mee and Kardinal Offishall to Drake's generation (glossary.md). Most of its words belong to those
   communities, and the game credits them by name (see section 7).

## 2. General Toronto / Canadian English

| Feature | What it is | Evidence | In the game |

### Prosody (our measurements, see section 5)
Read speech from 8 Toronto speakers comes out *close to* General American and Glasgow on rate and pausing. It
had slightly **less pitch movement** than the General American readers (9.3 vs 11.7 semitone range) and **more
phrase-final rises** (23% vs 12% of phrases), but the spread between speakers is wide (rises: sd 20%) and n = 8,
so treat both as tendencies, not findings. Uptalk in Canadian English is reported in the literature, but we did
not verify a Toronto-specific source tonight (UNVERIFIED).

## 3. Multicultural Toronto English (MTE)

### Sounds and rhythm
- **TH-stopping**: *thing* -> *ting*, *them* -> *dem*. Only about 10% of the time in interviews, led by teenage
  boys, but it is the most noticed MTE sound and the source of respellings like *ting* and *yute*
  (Bigelow et al. 2019, 2020).
- **GOAT as a monophthong**: *go* is [goː], not [goʊ], linked to Jamaican Patwa (Denis et al. 2023).
- **Weaker Canadian raising** in stereotyped words like *about*, kept in others (Denis et al. 2023; glossary).
- **Pre-nasal TRAP stays low**: *fam* -> *fom* (Elango & Denis 2021).
- **Rhythm**: described as leaning towards *syllable-timed*, like London's Multicultural London English
  (glossary; ASSUMPTION for Toronto: we could not measure an MTE clip tonight, see section 5).
- MTE is best described as a **register or feature pool** that speakers move in and out of, not as a fixed accent.
  Speakers of every background use it, and how much they use varies a lot from speaker to speaker
  (Denis et al. 2023; Denis et al. 2025, "If ur from Toronto you'll understand").

Dated or off: *T-dot* and *Hogtown* sound dated; "hang a larry" (Duolingo's invented Canadian slang) was roasted
by Torontonians (NOW Toronto, May 2026). Words drawn from religion, addiction or violence (*wallahi*, *bucktee*,
anything about crime) are explained in this study but **not taught as jokes** in the game.

## 4. What this means for the game

- **Unit 1, "Sound like a Torontonian"** (6 lessons): Trawna, raising in *out and about*, raising in *right* and
  *nice*, *sorry* and the cot-caught merger, the real *eh*, and street names (Spadina, Yonge). *Washroom*,
  *double-double* and *pop* are practised in Bev's coffee-counter quest.
- **Unit 2, "Talk like the 6ix"** (4 lessons): mans and mandem; ahlie, styll and bare; wagwan, say less and fam;
  and the street rhythm (TH-stopping in *ting*, *dem*). Each comes with its meaning, where it comes from and when it
  fits. Every lesson says the joke is on the tourist.
- **"Prove You're From the 6ix"**: the end-of-unit test, 6 lines from both units.
- **Scoring**: the in-browser matcher accepts common spellings and mishearings (Trawna/Tronna/Tranna,
  ahlie/allie/ah lie), see `src/game/torontoWords.js`. Vowel-quality scoring (raising, the Canadian Shift) is
  beyond the current matcher, so lessons teach those sounds through tips and listening rather than scores.
- **The NPCs**: Rocco (raccoon, general Toronto), Bev (beaver, coffee counter, standard Canadian), Gus (goose,
  streetcar driver), Dre (black squirrel, basketball court, light MTE). Only Dre uses MTE, because it is a youth
  register.

## 5. Acoustics (what we actually measured)

**Clips**: 21 Speech Accent Archive readings of the same paragraph ("Please call Stella..."): 8 born in Toronto,
8 General American (Ohio, Iowa, Nebraska), 5 born in Glasgow, plus the game's Glasgow otter TTS lines
([`acoustics/clips.json`](acoustics/clips.json), CC BY-NC-SA 4.0). The audio was downloaded to a temp dir and
deleted after measuring; no audio is committed. **YouTube refused all downloads from the research machine**
("Sign in to confirm you're not a bot"), so **no MTE clip and no spontaneous speech was measured**. Candidate MTE
clips with timestamps are flagged `acoustic_candidate: true` in sources.json, and
`measure.py --youtube URL 0:10-1:40 mte` runs them from any machine that YouTube does not block.

**Method** (`acoustics/measure.py`): Praat via parselmouth. F0 with sex-specific ranges; pauses = silences
≥150 ms at −25 dB; syllables from intensity peaks (after de Jong & Wempe 2009); phrase-final contour = the last
200 ms of each stretch between pauses against the 400 ms before it (rise > +1 st, fall < −1 st). Rhythm metrics
(%V, ΔC, nPVI-V) use a **voicing proxy** (voiced stretches stand in for vowels) because we had no forced aligner,
so compare the groups with each other only.

**Results** ([`acoustics/summary.md`](acoustics/summary.md), charts in `acoustics/charts/`):

| | Toronto (n=8) | General American (n=8) | Glasgow (n=5) | Glasgow otter TTS |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| F0 range, st (5-95%) | 9.3 | 11.7 | 9.9 | 19.9 |
| Speech rate, syll/s | 2.78 | 2.97 | 2.91 | 2.61 |
| Mean pause, s | 0.42 | 0.37 | 0.42 | 0.46 |
| %V / ΔC / nPVI-V (proxy) | 65.7 / 62 ms / 59.3 | 68.2 / 58 ms / 58.1 | 64.3 / 57 ms / 58.1 | 76.2 / 136 ms / 75.1 |
| Phrase-final rises / falls | 23% / 49% | 12% / 54% | 14% / 61% | 20% / 68% |

What it says:
- Read Toronto speech is **not rhythmically distinct** from General American by these measures. That fits the
  literature: Toronto stands out in a few vowels and words, not in rhythm. So the Toronto voice lines should be
  General North American in rhythm, with the vowels carried by the voice design and the tips.
- The **otter TTS is much more animated** than real Glaswegian readers (about twice the pitch range, larger ΔC).
  That is the cartoon style, and Toronto TTS lines will probably sound the same way (ASSUMPTION).
- **Vowel formants failed the sanity check** (for example *call* came out with F2 > 2000 Hz, impossible for that
  vowel). Automatic word windows plus formant tracking on these MP3s are not reliable, so the numbers are kept but
  labelled DO NOT USE, and every vowel claim in this study comes from the published studies. Fix next time:
  Montreal Forced Aligner and hand-checked tokens.
- The passage has no voiceless-context PRICE or MOUTH words, so it cannot show Canadian raising. Next time record
  or find "about, house, out, right, ice, like".

## 7. Respect

- The joke is always on the **tourist** (the player) getting it wrong, never on Torontonians or the communities
  the slang comes from. NPC prompts forbid crime, gangs, guns, drugs, poverty, religion and politics.
- MTE words are credited to their communities (Jamaican, Somali, Arabic, South Asian, West African) in the
  lesson text and in the NPC prompt, and they are taught as a living youth register, not as "how Toronto Black
  people talk".
- Oaths and insults with heavy meaning (*wallahi*, *bucktee*, *wasteman*) are explained here but kept out of
  the drills.
- **Everything needs native review** before launch: at least one Torontonian who grew up with MTE and one
  speaker of standard Toronto English should check every line. Sadman's U of T network is the obvious place to
  ask. Every content file carries `"review": "pending native review"`.
