# Toronto Slang Glossary — sourced

Compiled 2026-11-15. Almost every word here comes from the Caribbean (especially Jamaican Patwa), Somali, Arabic, South Asian, and West African communities of Toronto's inner suburbs — Jane-Finch, Rexdale, Malvern/Scarborough — and was popularized by the city's hip-hop scene (Michie Mee, Kardinal Offishall, Dream Warriors, then Drake's generation). Linguists call the variety **Multicultural Toronto English (MTE)**. Respect note (per VICE and The Walrus): this lexicon belongs to Black and racialized youth communities; mainstream use without credit is a live controversy, and speakers routinely code-switch because of accent stigma.

## Core terms

**mans** — first-person pronoun "I/me" (grammaticalized), also "my friend" or "people," per Derek Denis (TWPL 2016). Earliest UD attestation "manz" 2006, Twitter 2010; development parallel to London "man" but likely independent via shared Jamaican Creole roots.
- Example: "I almost missed the TTC but mans made it here anyways." (Denis)
- Sources: https://twpl.library.utoronto.ca/index.php/twpl/article/download/26973/20126/ , https://magazine.utoronto.ca/research-ideas/culture-society/do-you-know-toronto-slang/

**ahlie** — confirmational tag, "right?" / "is it true?", from Jamaican Patwa *a lie?*. Denis notes it "functions similarly to *eh* in normative Canadian English."
- Example: "You're reaching my crib tonight ahlie?" (UD)
- Sources: https://languagemuseum.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/A-Dictionary-of-English-in-Multicultural-Toronto.pdf , https://www.theglobeandmail.com/canada/article-toronto-slang-on-the-rise-thanks-to-citys-growing-pop-culture/ , https://www.urbandictionary.com/define.php?term=ahlie

**wallahi** — "I swear (to God)," from Somali via Arabic *wallāhi*. Heavy Somali contribution via Rexdale.
- Sources: https://magazine.utoronto.ca/research-ideas/culture-society/do-you-know-toronto-slang/ , https://www.vice.com/en/article/drake-torontos-slang-isnt-new-its-black/

**styll** — utterance-final concessive, roughly "though" / "still."
- Examples: "It was good, styll." / "Mans out here are fine, styll."
- Sources: https://cla-acl.ca/pdfs/actes-2021/Elango_and_Denis-FOM_and_friends.pdf , https://thevarsity.ca/2016/10/23/a-brief-guide-to-turrono-slang/ , https://www.urbandictionary.com/define.php?term=styll

**ting** — "thing," a person (often a woman) or casual relationship; Jamaican Patwa *ting*. The respelling <ting> indexes TH-stopping, a stereotyped MTE sound (~10% stopping in interviews, teenage males lead — Bigelow et al. 2020).
- Example: "All the tings are at Caribana fom you down?" (Narcity)
- Sources: https://repository.upenn.edu/pwpl/vol26/iss2/3 , https://www.narcity.com/toronto/40-things-youll-hear-a-scarberian-say

**bare** — "many/a lot," intensifier. Shared with UK slang; not Toronto-unique.
- Example: "Yo bare sweeties at Eaton."
- Sources: https://www.narcity.com/toronto/toronto-slang-words , https://slangiify.com/toronto-slang/

**fam / fom** — close friend/group. The spelling *fom/fham* is argued (Elango & Denis 2021) to index a laxer pre-nasal /æ/ — a subtle MTE vowel feature.
- Example: "What's up, fam?"
- Sources: https://cla-acl.ca/pdfs/actes-2021/Elango_and_Denis-FOM_and_friends.pdf , https://torontomansdictionary.com/

**wasteman / waste yute** — a useless person; *yute* < Jamaican Patwa *youth*. Bigelow et al. show the <yute> respelling enregisters DH-stopping.
- Example: "Stop mall ratting ya waste yute!!"
- Sources: https://repository.upenn.edu/pwpl/vol26/iss2/3 , https://www.urbandictionary.com/define.php?term=waste%20yute

**say less** — "understood; no need to explain," = "say no more." Roy Woods (2017 album) attributes it to Toronto street slang.
- Example: "Say less, I'll be there."
- Sources: https://languagelog.ldc.upenn.edu/nll/?p=54188 , https://slanginsights.com/toronto-slang/

**bucktee** — general pejorative (bum/crackhead/loser); from Somali *bukti* for drug addict. Famous in mainstream when the Raptors ran "Milwaukee Bucktees" signage (2019).
- Example: "Yo I can't go to the Timmies on Dufferin again fam there's always this bucktee outside yelling at mans across the road!" (UD)
- Sources: https://www.theglobeandmail.com/canada/article-toronto-slang-on-the-rise-thanks-to-citys-growing-pop-culture/ , https://www.urbandictionary.com/define.php?term=bucktee

**you're moving funny / moving sus** — "you're acting suspicious/shady." Attested in Toronto-rap usage; treat as current collocation (sources thin — one lyrics snippet in search results, no opened primary source).
- Sources: (weak) https://slanginsights.com/toronto-slang/ for the "moving + adj." frame ("He's moving bucktee today")

**u dun know / dun kno** — "you already know," emphatic agreement; Patois-derived, in Toronto rap since Kardinal Offishall's *BaKardi Slang* (2000): "T-dot say's Yuh dun know."
- Sources: https://languagemuseum.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/A-Dictionary-of-English-in-Multicultural-Toronto.pdf , https://www.narcity.com/toronto/toronto-slang-words

**mandem / man dem** — "the guys," one's male crew; Jamaican Patwa *man dem*. Attested in Michie Mee lyrics (1991).
- Sources: https://languagemuseum.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/A-Dictionary-of-English-in-Multicultural-Toronto.pdf , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_slang

**the ends / endz** — one's neighbourhood, home turf.
- Example: "Come through the endz this weekend." (Toronto Mans Dictionary)
- Sources: https://torontomansdictionary.com/ , https://www.urbandictionary.com/define.php?term=the%20ends

**wagwan / wah gwan** — "what's going on?" Jamaican Patwa greeting; Rexdale Somali kids use it too per the Globe.
- Example: "Wagwan my G?"
- Sources: https://www.theglobeandmail.com/news/toronto/popularization-of-patois-stirs-up-complex-feelings-for-torontos-westindiancommunity/article35884529/ , https://www.narcity.com/toronto/toronto-slang-words

**cheesed** — annoyed/angry.
- Sources: https://thevarsity.ca/2016/10/23/a-brief-guide-to-turrono-slang/ , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_slang

**flex** — show off; also gym/weightlifting context in Toronto usage.
- Sources: https://torontomansdictionary.com/ , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_slang

**bean** — uncertain in Toronto slang lists; UD waste-yute dialogue uses "Dont be a bean" (= lame person). Not well attested — treat as marginal. Source: https://www.urbandictionary.com/define.php?term=waste%20yute

**allow it / 'llow it** — "let it go, drop it." Source: https://torontomansdictionary.com/

**nize it** — "shut up, stop it" (clipping of "recognize" via Patwa *nize* = noise).
- Sources: https://languagemuseum.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/A-Dictionary-of-English-in-Multicultural-Toronto.pdf , https://magazine.utoronto.ca/research-ideas/culture-society/do-you-know-toronto-slang/

**my guy** — friend/familiar address ("my guy" appears in UD example sentences); common North American vocative used heavily in Toronto slang registers. Sources: https://www.urbandictionary.com/define.php?term=ahlie

**skeen** — agreed / understood / "whatever" (dismissive); twist on Jamaican *seen*. Source: https://properslang.com/articles/toronto-slang-guide

**gyaldem** — girls/women (counterpart to mandem). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_slang

**two-twos** — in a moment / suddenly. Source: https://nowtoronto.com/culture/torontonians-are-roasting-duolingos-take-on-canadian-slang/

**crodie/brodie** — close friend. Sources: https://torontomansdictionary.com/ , https://nowtoronto.com/culture/torontonians-are-roasting-duolingos-take-on-canadian-slang/

**the 6ix** — Toronto nickname; coined by rapper Jimmy Prime, popularized by Drake's *Views From the 6* (title announced July 14, 2014). Refers to 416/647 area codes and/or the six pre-amalgamation municipalities. Older nicknames: T-dot, T.O., Hogtown.
- Sources: https://thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/drake , https://www.theglobeandmail.com/news/toronto/we-the-6-why-the-name-drake-gave-us-is-here-to-stay/article25421112/

**marved** — starving/hungry. Source: https://thevarsity.ca/2016/10/23/a-brief-guide-to-turrono-slang/

**miskeen** — "poor thing" (Arabic), used sympathetically or patronizingly. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_slang

**aboki** — friend/homie (Hausa). Source: https://languagemuseum.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/A-Dictionary-of-English-in-Multicultural-Toronto.pdf

**dukes** — parents. Source: https://languagemuseum.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/A-Dictionary-of-English-in-Multicultural-Toronto.pdf

**from time** — "since way back / for a while" (Patwa). Source: https://www.theglobeandmail.com/news/toronto/popularization-of-patois-stirs-up-complex-feelings-for-torontos-westindiancommunity/article35884529/

**marites** — gossip/gossiper (Tagalog). Source: https://languagemuseum.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/A-Dictionary-of-English-in-Multicultural-Toronto.pdf

## MTE sounds & rhythm (for the accent)

- **TH/DH-stopping**: *ting* < *thing*, *dem* < *them* — about 10% in interviews, led by teenage males; now enregistered via respellings. (Bigelow et al. 2020)
- **GOAT monophthongization**: *go* → [go:] not [goʊ], traced to Jamaican Patwa. (Denis et al. 2023)
- **Weakened Canadian raising**: MTE speakers avoid raising in stereotype-loaded words like *about* (no "aboot") but keep it in ice/eyes.
- **Unraised pre-nasal TRAP**: *fam* stays lax → respelled *fom/fham*. (Elango & Denis 2021)
- **GOOSE fronting** and participation in the Canadian Shift, but with high interspeaker variability — MTE is best described as a "feature pool"/register, not a fixed dialect. (Denis et al. 2023, J Engl Ling)
- Rhythm tends toward syllable-timed like other multiethnolects (comparison: Multicultural London English).

## Dated vs current quick guide

- **Current**: mans, ahlie, wallahi, styll, bare, fam, wasteman, say less, the ends, wagwan, cheesed, nize it, crodie, skeen, my guy, two-twos.
- **Established/older but still understood**: ting, mandem, gyaldem, waste yute, dun know, from time, marved (2015–2016 lists feature them all).
- **Dating yourself**: T-dot, Hogtown (historical), "Toronto mans" as a jokey label is mainstream, not in-group.
- **Fake/off**: "hang a larry/roger" (Duolingo got roasted for inventing usage — NOW Toronto, May 2026).
