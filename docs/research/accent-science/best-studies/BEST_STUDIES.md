# Best accent-science studies for Sound Local

Access date 2026-11-19. This adds **139 studies** that weren't in `sources.json`: 80 on North American regional accents and 59 on others (Toronto/MTE, Glasgow, Indian, London/MLE, accent-distance methods, measurement on phones).
- **Links:** I checked every one by script. DOIs were matched against Crossref (title and year) and the other URLs returned HTTP 200.
- **Access:** 66 have a free copy. The rest say "UofT library".
- **Numbers:** they come from abstracts or open full texts, and anything unchecked says UNVERIFIED in the full tables.
- **Ranking:** 12 studies are rated 5/5 for evidence. Within each region, the "read first" studies are listed in order.

Files:
- `na_regional_studies.md`: the full North America table, with datasets and licences.
- `other_studies.md`: everything else.
- `studies.json`: all 139 with every field.

## North America: what to score, and the studies behind it
For each region: the sounds you can graph and score, real numbers, a lesson hook, and the studies to read first.

### New York City
- **Score:** dropped "r" after vowels (*fourth floor*), raised THOUGHT (*coffee, talk*: first formant F1 below about 700 Hz, NYC talkers 550-655 Hz), and the short-a split (*bad* tense vs *bat* lax).
- **Real numbers:** Saks staff using only full "r" went from 29% (1963) to 78% (2017), and Macy's from 20% to 57%. Lower East Side speakers say "r" 68% of the time. So a modern New Yorker drops it about a third of the time, and dropping every "r" sounds old-school.
- **Lesson idea:** the department-store lift ("Which floor?" "Fourth floor."), the exact phrase from Labov's 1960s study.
- **Read first:** [Gregory R. Guy 2018](https://repository.upenn.edu/items/a4084623-964e-4bbe-8725-6210c35a61fb) ([free](https://repository.upenn.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/daa7fbee-cca0-4473-a72b-7ea7ce4793a2/content)), [Kara Becker 2014](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0954394514000064) (UofT library), [Bill Haddican et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0954394521000120) (UofT library), [William Labov 2006](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511618208) (UofT library).

### Chicago and the Inland North
- **Score:** Northern Cities Shift vowels, mainly raised TRAP (*bag, Dad*, toward "bee-ag") and fronted LOT (*hot dog* toward "hat dag"). Plot each on a vowel map.
- **Watch out:** the shift is reversing in younger speakers in Chicago, Lansing and Syracuse. "Local" should mean the classic older pattern, labelled as such.
- **Lesson idea:** ordering a hot dog "with everything but ketchup".
- **Read first:** [Annette D'Onofrio 2019](https://doi.org/10.1111/josl.12398) (UofT library), [Monica Nesbitt 2018](https://repository.upenn.edu/items/ebec219b-be3b-4da8-9bb5-55a5251986f7) ([free](https://repository.upenn.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/8d304577-3a11-43c9-a3c7-2da0e0094de8/content)), [Anna Driscoll 2015](https://repository.upenn.edu/items/16353006-2549-475f-86b6-f1545595ed20) ([free](https://repository.upenn.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/9696a029-3eb3-4417-8d6f-58347f9d49a9/content)). The ANAE is already in PR #34.

### Boston and Eastern New England
- **Score:** dropped "r" (*park the car*). Boston speakers say "r" about 38% of the time overall, about 66% for highly educated speakers, and under 35% among young Black Bostonians.
- **Watch out:** these traditional features are receding.
- **Lesson idea:** parking near Fenway.
- **Read first:** [Patricia Irwin 2007](https://repository.upenn.edu/items/4e60588f-b3ab-453e-8b13-b0ec04719ed1) ([free](https://repository.upenn.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/d0d4671a-a995-4978-8056-793ba331d3de/content)), [Naomi Nagy 2010](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0954394510000062) (UofT library), [James N. Stanford 2019](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780190625658.001.0001) (UofT library).

### Atlanta and the South
- **Score:** PRICE without the glide (*nice white rice* toward "nahs waht rahs"), shown as the vowel's movement path (20%-80% of the vowel), not a single midpoint. Also longer vowels and the drawl.
- **Real numbers:** 36% of 59 Atlanta speakers had the glide-less PRICE (59% of Black vs 17% of white speakers). The South has the longest vowels of three regions studied. Drawled vowels last 374-411 ms.
- **Watch out:** urban Raleigh has moved away from the Southern Shift over three generations.
- **Lesson idea:** sweet tea and "fixin' to" at a Waffle House-style diner.
- **Read first:** [Margaret E. L. Renwick 2020](https://doi.org/10.1121/10.0000549) ([free](https://asa.scitation.org/doi/pdf/10.1121/10.0000549)), [Hilary Prichard 2010](https://repository.upenn.edu/items/9dea5066-93fe-4988-b588-98063066dfec) ([free](https://repository.upenn.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/a12d14b5-ade4-45ef-bbdb-9c0b30fae008/content)), [Valerie Fridland 2003](https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-9481.00225) (UofT library), [Robin Dodsworth 2012](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0954394512000105) (UofT library).

### Philadelphia
- **Score:** the short-a split (*mad, bad* vs *sad*), measured per speaker with Pillai, a 0-1 score of how far apart two vowels sit. Also fronted /aw/ and /ow/ (*down, go*) and raised /ay/ before voiceless consonants (*fight* vs *five*).
- **Real numbers:** 379 Philadelphia Neighborhood Corpus speakers born 1888-1991. /aw/ and /ow/ fronting reverses for those born after 1940.
- **Lesson idea:** a cheesesteak counter.
- **Read first:** [William Labov et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1353/lan.2013.0015) ([free](https://www.research.ed.ac.uk/files/13269097/One_Hundred_Years_of_Sound_Change_in_Philadelphia.pdf)), [William Labov et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0954394516000132) (UofT library).

### Pittsburgh
- **Score:** /aw/ as a plain vowel (*downtown* sounding like "dahntahn"), using the length of the vowel's movement path (shorter means more local).
- **Watch out:** both sources describe what the feature means locally rather than measuring it. No numbers were checked.
- **Read first:** [Barbara Johnstone et al. 2006](https://doi.org/10.1177/0075424206290692) ([free](https://figshare.com/articles/journal_contribution/Mobility_Indexicality_and_the_Enregisterment_of_Pittsburghese_/6469676)), [Barbara Johnstone 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9841.2008.00351.x) (UofT library).

### California and the West
- **Score:** the cot/caught merger (Pillai near 0 means merged), lowered and backed TRAP, fronted GOOSE (*dude*).
- **Watch out:** one study of 72 Californians finds the vowel space squeezed together rather than a chain shift.
- **Read first:** [Robert J. Podesva et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1017/S095439451500006X) (UofT library), [Lauren Hall-Lew 2013](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1360674313000063) ([free](https://hdl.handle.net/20.500.11820/10eba9a4-b61c-4c97-8525-e819053bbb82)).

### Canada and Toronto (incl. MTE)
- **Score:** Canadian raising (*about, house* before voiceless consonants, measured by lower F1) and the Canadian Shift (TRAP pulled back, DRESS and KIT lowered).
- **Watch out:** the Canadian Shift resembles California's, so it is a good test that the scorer doesn't confuse the two.
- **MTE:** no acoustic study with numbers exists yet. The best sources treat it as a speech style young people switch into, so Sound Local would need its own consented recordings.
- **Read first:** [J. K. Chambers 1973](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0008413100007350) (UofT library), [Charles Boberg 2010](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511781056) (UofT library), [Denis et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1017/s1360674324000509) ([free](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/BB67861CE19ACA8A38FA4EA413560A61/S1360674324000509a.pdf/div-class-title-if-ur-from-toronto-you-ll-understand-register-change-and-metadiscursive-engagement-with-mediatized-multicultural-toronto-english-div.pdf)), [Hoffman 2010](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0954394509990238) (UofT library).

### African American English
- **Findings:** a distinct vowel system and question intonation across CORAAL sites.
- **Real numbers:** five commercial speech recognisers made errors on 35% of words for Black speakers vs 19% for white speakers.
- **Product point:** never score with speech-to-text accuracy alone.
- **Content point:** this needs care. The game's rule is "kind, never mocking", so AAE shouldn't become a goofy imitation unit without community input.
- **Read first:** [Allison Koenecke et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1915768117) ([free](https://www.pnas.org/content/pnas/117/14/7684.full.pdf)), [Erik R. Thomas 2006](https://doi.org/10.1075/eww.27.3.06tho) (UofT library), [Nicole R. Holliday 2019](https://doi.org/10.1215/00031283-7308038) (UofT library).

### Rhythm, speed and melody across US regions
- **Real numbers:** Wisconsin speakers talk faster than North Carolina speakers (192 speakers). Listeners can group talkers by region from melody alone, but naive listeners are poor at a 6-way region test.
- **Read first:** [Ewa Jacewicz et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.3459842) ([free](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2933259/)), [Cynthia G. Clopper 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.wocn.2014.10.002) (UofT library), [Cynthia G. Clopper 2007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.wocn.2006.06.001) ([free](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3059784/)), [Cynthia G. Clopper et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.2000774) ([free](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/3432912)).

## How to measure reliably on a phone (applies to every region)
1. **Normalise vowels per speaker** with Lobanov or Watt-Fabricius, so a deep and a high voice can be compared. Picking a method: [Anne H. Fabricius et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0954394509990160) ([free](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/68946/1/fabricius_et_al_2009.pdf)). Normalising across different recording setups: [Rathcke et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.specom.2016.11.001) ([free](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/58978/1/Rathckeetal2016_SpeechComm_authormanuscript.pdf)).
2. **Phones are fine, video calls aren't.** Pitch tracked accurately on every device tested, and phones came closer than Zoom to a studio recorder for formants: [Zhang et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1121/10.0005132) ([free](https://asa.scitation.org/doi/pdf/10.1121/10.0005132)). Zoom lowered raw F1 and raised F2, even after normalising: [Calder et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1515/lingvan-2020-0148) (UofT library). Recording devices and apps change the numbers: [Sanker et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1353/lan.2021.0075) (UofT library).
3. **Telephone audio raises measured F1**, so set the vowel targets on the same kind of audio players record: [Kunzel 2001](https://doi.org/10.1558/ijsll.v8i1.80) (UofT library).
4. **Formant tracking:** [Santiago Barreda 2021](https://doi.org/10.1515/lingvan-2020-0051) (UofT library) picks the smoothest track automatically. Errors grow with higher voices: [Shadle et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.4940665) ([free](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/4752539)).
5. **Pitch in noise:** [Gonzalez 2014](https://doi.org/10.1109/TASLP.2013.2295918) (UofT library).
6. **Use vowel paths for vowels that glide** (Southern PRICE, Pittsburgh MOUTH) and **Pillai for mergers** (cot/caught, short-a).
7. **Ceiling:** treat human listeners' accuracy at naming the region as the most the game should claim: [Cynthia G. Clopper 2004](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0095-4470(03)00009-3) ([free](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3065110/)). Automatic accent distance checked against listeners: [Huckvale 2004](https://doi.org/10.21437/interspeech.2004-29) ([free](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/12139/1/12139.pdf)), [Gooskens 2004](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0954394504163023) (UofT library).

## Gaps
- **Pittsburgh, Philadelphia /ay/ and the Canadian Shift:** several numbers are still UNVERIFIED, and the full texts are paywalled.
- **MTE:** no study has published acoustic numbers.
- **Training data:** the best regional corpora (Nationwide Speech Project, TIMIT, the Southern speech archive) are paid LDC licences, and CORAAL and Buckeye are non-commercial. None can train a model we ship without a licence.
