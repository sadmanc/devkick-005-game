# North American regional accent studies for Sound Local

Access date for every link: 2026-11-19. 80 entries; the JSON has full fields. **OA** = free copy linked; **Paywalled** = use the UofT library.

Rules followed:
- Numbers come only from the abstract or open full text I read; anything not checked says UNVERIFIED.
- DOIs were confirmed through the Crossref API. doi.org returns 403 to scripts because publishers block bots; the links work in a browser.
- OA status is from Unpaywall or the repository record.
- Entries already in `docs/research/accent-science/sources.json` were excluded: ANAE, Boberg 2008, Roeder & Jarmasz 2010, Clarke et al. 1995, Fox & Jacewicz 2009, Hillenbrand, and the datasets.

## New York City (10)

**Signature features you can graph**

- Coda /r/: % r-less in coda; department-store "fourth floor" r-ful-only rose from 29% to 78% (Saks) and 20% to 57% (Macy's), 1963 to 2017. Becker (2014) found 68% r-ful on the Lower East Side.
- Raised THOUGHT: ANAE threshold F1 < 700 Hz; six NYC talkers had means of 550-655 Hz. It lowers with later birth year.
- Short-a split: tense environments have lower F1 than lax ones (about 507-700 Hz across environments). Younger and minority New Yorkers increasingly use a nasal system.
- PRICE fronting vs LOT-PALM overlap (Pillai). Borough-level ID is not feasible, even for New York listeners.

- **The Social Stratification of English in New York City**: William Labov (2006), *Cambridge University Press*.
  - Link: [doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511618208](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511618208). Paywalled. Strength 5/5.
  - **Finding:** Foundational NYC study (2006 2nd edition of the 1966 book): coda /r/ stratified by social class and speech style, with the department-store survey; other NYC vowels (short-a, THOUGHT) described. Specific percentages: UNVERIFIED.
  - **Graph:** Bar chart: % coda [r] by class x style (casual -> word list); NYC r-less is the local value
  - **Why it matters:** Defines the three classic NYC markers to score: r-dropping, raised BAD/short-a, raised THOUGHT.
- **Saks vs. Macys: (r-1) marches on in New York City department stores**: Gregory R. Guy (2018), *University of Pennsylvania Working Papers in Linguistics (ScholarlyCommons/UPenn repository)*.
  - Link: [repository.upenn.edu/items/a4084623-964e-4bbe-8725-6210c35a61fb](https://repository.upenn.edu/items/a4084623-964e-4bbe-8725-6210c35a61fb). OA: [free copy](https://repository.upenn.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/daa7fbee-cca0-4473-a72b-7ea7ce4793a2/content). Strength 4/5.
  - **Finding:** 54-year trend extension of Labov's department-store study: Saks employees using only r-1 rose from 29% (1963) to 78% (2017); Macy's from 20% to 57%. In 1963 "floor" had about double the r-1 rate of "fourth" (44% vs 22% Macy's; 63% vs 30% Saks).
  - **Graph:** Line chart of % r-ful by year (1963, 1986, 2009, 2017); a "localness" r-score should treat r-lessness as declining/older-sounding
  - **Why it matters:** Direct calibration for the NYC r-lessness scorer and a ready-made elicitation phrase ("fourth floor").
- **The Social Stratification of /r/ in New York City**: Patrick-André Mather (2011), *Journal of English Linguistics*.
  - Link: [doi.org/10.1177/0075424211431265](https://doi.org/10.1177/0075424211431265). Paywalled. Strength 4/5.
  - **Finding:** 169 participants (June 2009) in a replica of Labov's department-store study; overall r-ful percentages up by about 10-20 points versus earlier surveys; younger lower-middle-class speakers still use r-less forms.
  - **Graph:** % r-1 by store x year (1962/1986/2009)
  - **Why it matters:** Shows modern NYC r-lessness is real but minority; set the "local" bonus accordingly.
- **(r) we there yet? The change to rhoticity in New York City English**: Kara Becker (2014), *Language Variation and Change*.
  - Link: [doi.org/10.1017/S0954394514000064](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0954394514000064). Paywalled. Strength 4/5.
  - **Finding:** 65 Lower East Side speakers; mean /r/ production rate 68% (abstract). Change toward rhoticity continues slowly.
  - **Graph:** % coda [r] by birth decade; NYC typical ~68% r-ful in conversation
  - **Why it matters:** Gives a realistic modern baseline: an all-r-less NYC player is "old-school"; ~30% r-lessness is typical.
- **Aspects of change in New York City English short-a**: Bill Haddican, Michael Newman, Cecelia Cutler et al. (2021), *Language Variation and Change*.
  - Link: [doi.org/10.1017/S0954394521000120](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0954394521000120). Paywalled. Strength 4/5.
  - **Finding:** At least two separate changes: prenasal short-a and pre-oral obstruent contexts (including "trap-backing") change differently; the traditional split is receding. Effect sizes: UNVERIFIED.
  - **Graph:** Short-a F1/F2 by birth year, split by prenasal vs pre-oral
  - **Why it matters:** Tells you which NYC short-a cues mark older vs younger New Yorkers.
- **Cross-Speaker Covariation across Six Vocalic Changes in New York City English**: Bill Haddican, Cecelia Cutler, Michael Newman et al. (2022), *American Speech*.
  - Link: [doi.org/10.1215/00031283-9616153](https://doi.org/10.1215/00031283-9616153). Paywalled. Strength 4/5.
  - **Finding:** Corpus of conversational speech from 140 speakers; covariation across six vocalic changes is conditioned by their local social embedding. Formant values: UNVERIFIED.
  - **Graph:** Per-speaker correlation matrix of six vowel indices
  - **Why it matters:** A multi-feature NYC score should not assume features move together.
- **The Short-a System of New York City English: An Update**: Kara Becker, Amy Wing-mei Wong (2010), *University of Pennsylvania Working Papers in Linguistics (ScholarlyCommons/UPenn repository)*.
  - Link: [repository.upenn.edu/items/7ed8ed5a-c3a9-4a87-8f36-21a7f0281c97](https://repository.upenn.edu/items/7ed8ed5a-c3a9-4a87-8f36-21a7f0281c97). OA: [free copy](https://repository.upenn.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/33b9604c-3758-416b-be44-2a5e9da3c3e8/content). Strength 3/5.
  - **Finding:** Measured F0, F1, F2, F3 at onset (35 ms) and offset plus duration for each short-a token; table reports environment mean F1 values from about 507 to 700 Hz (exact environment-to-value mapping UNVERIFIED: PDF table text garbled). Young ethnic-minority native New Yorkers (Chinese, Puerto Rican, African American) produce a nasal tensing system, not the traditional NYC split.
  - **Graph:** Short-a F1 by following segment: tense (raised) NYC environments have lower F1 than lax ones; range ~507-700 Hz across environments
  - **Why it matters:** Best open source of actual Hz values for the NYC short-a split; lets the game test BAD vs BAT contrast.
- **Almost everyone in New York is raising PRICEs**: Michael Newman, Bill Haddican, Zi Zi Gina Tan (2018), *University of Pennsylvania Working Papers in Linguistics (ScholarlyCommons/UPenn repository)*.
  - Link: [repository.upenn.edu/items/28f8efa8-72e5-47a4-ab76-8445183bd290](https://repository.upenn.edu/items/28f8efa8-72e5-47a4-ab76-8445183bd290). OA: [free copy](https://repository.upenn.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/27d683aa-49d7-4858-a382-9791b7de6e47/content). Strength 3/5.
  - **Finding:** CUNY Corpus of NYC English: for non-Black speakers, lower LOT-PALM Pillai (more overlap) goes with more PRICE fronting; Black New Yorkers show no such relationship.
  - **Graph:** Scatter: speaker PRICE F2 vs LOT-PALM Pillai score
  - **Why it matters:** Adds PRICE/PALM to the NYC feature set and warns of ethnic differences.
- **The Lowering of Raised-THOUGHT and the Low-Back Distinction in New York City: Evidence from Chinese Americans**: Amy Wing-mei Wong (2012), *University of Pennsylvania Working Papers in Linguistics (ScholarlyCommons/UPenn repository)*.
  - Link: [repository.upenn.edu/items/796eac43-b62a-4288-b9b3-6a14273dc651](https://repository.upenn.edu/items/796eac43-b62a-4288-b9b3-6a14273dc651). OA: [free copy](https://repository.upenn.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/2484820f-f4b4-4f63-a453-88e93612bf42/content). Strength 3/5.
  - **Finding:** 16 NYC-born Chinese American males aged 11-61; about 600 THOUGHT and LOT tokens measured; normalised F1 of THOUGHT lowers as year of birth increases (corroborating Becker 2010).
  - **Graph:** THOUGHT normalised F1 vs birth year (raised = older NYC)
  - **Why it matters:** Raised THOUGHT ("cawfee") is a strong but ageing NYC cue.
- **The Myth of the New York City Borough Accent: Evidence from Perception**: Kara Becker, Luiza Newlin-Lukowicz (2018), *University of Pennsylvania Working Papers in Linguistics (ScholarlyCommons/UPenn repository)*.
  - Link: [repository.upenn.edu/items/22118aa3-f75c-4e75-ba77-eec013de1d25](https://repository.upenn.edu/items/22118aa3-f75c-4e75-ba77-eec013de1d25). OA: [free copy](https://repository.upenn.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/62514fc2-2c1a-4709-807c-46757c995f71/content). Strength 3/5.
  - **Finding:** Six talkers all exceeded the ANAE BOUGHT-raising threshold (F1 < 700 Hz; means 550-655 Hz). Listeners, including New Yorkers, could not identify talkers' boroughs; only one talker got a majority (72%/77% of votes).
  - **Graph:** BOUGHT F1 < 700 Hz = raised (NYC); borough-level classification is not feasible
  - **Why it matters:** Do not promise "which borough"; NYC-level scoring is the right granularity. Gives a concrete THOUGHT threshold.

## Chicago & Inland North (Northern Cities Shift) (9)

**Signature features you can graph**

- NCS chain of six lax vowels /ae, o, oh, e, uh, ih/ (TRAP, LOT, THOUGHT, DRESS, STRUT, KIT); directions per ANAE in sources.json.
- Lansing TRAP reference lines are F1 700 Hz and F2 1855 Hz. Raised TRAP rose, then reversed with Baby Boomers.
- Reversal and reorganisation are shown in Chicago (36 and 42 speakers), Syracuse (54) and Lansing (27-50). Plot per-vowel F1/F2 vs birth year.
- Pre-/g/ raising ("bag") and an emerging low-back merger appear in Wisconsin and Michigan.

- **Transmission and diffusion**: William Labov (2007), *Language*.
  - Link: [doi.org/10.1353/lan.2007.0082](https://doi.org/10.1353/lan.2007.0082). Paywalled. Strength 4/5.
  - **Finding:** Transmission (within communities) vs diffusion (across); NYC short-a split did not diffuse intact, NCS diffused broadly. Numbers UNVERIFIED.
  - **Graph:** Map/flow diagram of feature diffusion
  - **Why it matters:** Explains why NCS features appear far from Chicago.
- **Contextualizing reversal: Local dynamics of the Northern Cities Shift in a Chicago community**: Annette D'Onofrio, Jaime Benheim (2019), *Journal of Sociolinguistics*.
  - Link: [doi.org/10.1111/josl.12398](https://doi.org/10.1111/josl.12398). Paywalled. Strength 4/5.
  - **Finding:** 42 White speakers in one Chicago community; NCS features reverse in apparent time but not uniformly. Formant numbers UNVERIFIED.
  - **Graph:** TRAP, LOT, THOUGHT F1/F2 by birth year (Chicago)
  - **Why it matters:** Young Chicagoans may sound less NCS; scoring should be age-aware.
- **The Northern Cities Shift in Chicago**: Corrine McCarthy (2010), *Journal of English Linguistics*.
  - Link: [doi.org/10.1177/0075424210384226](https://doi.org/10.1177/0075424210384226). Paywalled. Strength 4/5.
  - **Finding:** Acoustic data from 36 Chicago speakers: none of BAT, BOT, BOUGHT continues shifting in the expected direction; marginal BOT reversal; BUT/BOUGHT overlap.
  - **Graph:** Chicago vowel plot: raised/fronted BAT, fronted BOT, BUT backed toward BOUGHT
  - **Why it matters:** Defines which Chicago vowels are stable enough to score.
- **The Rise and Fall of the Northern Cities Shift**: Monica Nesbitt (2021), *American Speech*.
  - Link: [doi.org/10.1215/00031283-8791754](https://doi.org/10.1215/00031283-8791754). Paywalled. Strength 4/5.
  - **Finding:** 36 White Lansing, Michigan speakers (18 women, 18 men) across the 20th century: NCS raised TRAP rose then fell.
  - **Graph:** TRAP F1 vs birth year: raised then lowered
  - **Why it matters:** Classic NCS raised TRAP is now generational.
- **Economic Change and the Decline of Raised TRAP in Lansing, MI**: Monica Nesbitt (2018), *University of Pennsylvania Working Papers in Linguistics (ScholarlyCommons/UPenn repository)*.
  - Link: [repository.upenn.edu/items/ebec219b-be3b-4da8-9bb5-55a5251986f7](https://repository.upenn.edu/items/ebec219b-be3b-4da8-9bb5-55a5251986f7). OA: [free copy](https://repository.upenn.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/8d304577-3a11-43c9-a3c7-2da0e0094de8/content). Strength 3/5.
  - **Finding:** Lansing natives born 1907-1997 (N=27): NCS raised TRAP reverses in the Baby Boomer generation, with a class distinction emerging. Figure reference lines at 700 Hz (F1) and 1855 Hz (F2); one Boomer speaker had only 15% of TRAP tokens raised above her LOT cloud.
  - **Graph:** TRAP pre-nasal vs pre-oral Pillai by birth year; F1 ~700 Hz / F2 ~1855 Hz reference lines
  - **Why it matters:** Gives Hz reference lines for NCS vs non-NCS TRAP.
- **Reversal and Re-Organization of the Northern Cities Shift in Michigan**: Suzanne E Wagner, Alexander Mason, Monica Nesbitt et al. (2016), *University of Pennsylvania Working Papers in Linguistics (ScholarlyCommons/UPenn repository)*.
  - Link: [repository.upenn.edu/items/1cd242f8-0ffb-4718-93d4-90ad45dee0fd](https://repository.upenn.edu/items/1cd242f8-0ffb-4718-93d4-90ad45dee0fd). OA: [free copy](https://repository.upenn.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/9260018b-df99-41da-af26-595bcbd231ca/content). Strength 3/5.
  - **Finding:** 50 speakers born 1908-1996: NCS shows reversal, reorganisation and continuation; some young speakers cluster around 1300 Hz F2 for one vowel, 50-100 Hz backer than the comparison group (vowel: see paper).
  - **Graph:** Per-vowel F2 by birth year; TRAP Pillai by birth year
  - **Why it matters:** "Reversal" is not uniform; per-vowel scoring is safer.
- **Reversal of the Northern Cities Shift in Syracuse, New York**: Anna Driscoll, Emma Lape (2015), *University of Pennsylvania Working Papers in Linguistics (ScholarlyCommons/UPenn repository)*.
  - Link: [repository.upenn.edu/items/16353006-2549-475f-86b6-f1545595ed20](https://repository.upenn.edu/items/16353006-2549-475f-86b6-f1545595ed20). OA: [free copy](https://repository.upenn.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/9696a029-3eb3-4417-8d6f-58347f9d49a9/content). Strength 3/5.
  - **Finding:** 54 Syracuse interviews (ages 18-89), 14,026 tokens; NCS is reversing in Syracuse. Manual vs FAVE check on ~10% of speakers.
  - **Graph:** Normalised F1/F2 of /ae, o, oh, e, uh, ih/ by age group
  - **Why it matters:** Shows FAVE-style automatic measurement is good enough, which also suits a phone pipeline.
- **THE BAG THAT SCOTT Bought: THE LOW VOWELS IN NORTHWEST WISCONSIN**: Erica J. Benson, Michael J. Fox, Jared Balkman (2011), *American Speech*.
  - Link: [doi.org/10.1215/00031283-1503910](https://doi.org/10.1215/00031283-1503910). Paywalled. Strength 3/5.
  - **Finding:** 40 respondents in greater Eau Claire, NW Wisconsin: advanced /ae/ raising before /g/, moderate raising elsewhere, little /a/ fronting, low-back merger in progress.
  - **Graph:** "bag" raised vs "bat" lower; LOT/THOUGHT overlap
  - **Why it matters:** Pre-/g/ raising (bag) is a useful Upper Midwest cue.
- **The St. Louis Corridor: Mixing, Competing, and Retreating Dialects**: Lauren Friedman (2014), *PhD dissertation, University of Pennsylvania (UPenn repository)*.
  - Link: [repository.upenn.edu/items/243a1006-7c63-4758-a3bd-dd2b10b23eed](https://repository.upenn.edu/items/243a1006-7c63-4758-a3bd-dd2b10b23eed). OA: [free copy](https://repository.upenn.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/00c0cea2-27f0-4140-8eb5-144b294e1cc1/content). Strength 3/5.
  - **Finding:** Dissertation: NCS rose and retreated in the St. Louis Corridor, linked to Route 66 and population movement; data from new interviews and interviews as early as 1972. Numbers UNVERIFIED.
  - **Graph:** NCS index by birth year in St. Louis
  - **Why it matters:** Shows NCS-like sound outside the Inland North.

## Boston & Eastern New England (7)

**Signature features you can graph**

- Coda /r/: overall 38% [r] (about 62% r-less) in Boston. Rates vary by community; under 35% r-less among young Black Bostonians.
- The traditional Eastern New England features (r-lessness and others) are receding. The east-west New England contrast is fading.
- In Western Massachusetts, the Low-Back-Merger Shift is replacing NCS and older New England features.

- **New England English**: James N. Stanford (2019), *Oxford University PressNew York*.
  - Link: [doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780190625658.001.0001](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780190625658.001.0001). Paywalled. Strength 5/5.
  - **Finding:** Book: data from over 1,600 New Englanders (367 field interviews, 626 audio-recorded interviews, 634 online questionnaires).
  - **Graph:** Maps of feature rates across six states
  - **Why it matters:** The broadest New England source.
- **Boston (r): Neighbo(r)s nea(r) and fa(r)**: Naomi Nagy, Patricia Irwin (2010), *Language Variation and Change*.
  - Link: [doi.org/10.1017/S0954394510000062](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0954394510000062). Paywalled. Strength 4/5.
  - **Finding:** Boston (r) patterns fit transmission (Boston) and diffusion (New Hampshire) only with accommodation theory. Rates UNVERIFIED.
  - **Graph:** % r-less by city and age
  - **Why it matters:** Supports a Boston r-less score that changes with age.
- **Multiple vectors of unidirectional dialect change in eastern New England**: James N. Stanford, Nathan A. Severance, Kenneth P. Baclawski (2014), *Language Variation and Change*.
  - Link: [doi.org/10.1017/S0954394513000227](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0954394513000227). Paywalled. Strength 4/5.
  - **Finding:** Eastern New England traditional features are receding along several dimensions. Numbers UNVERIFIED.
  - **Graph:** Traditional-feature rate by birth year (e.g. % r-less)
  - **Why it matters:** Older Boston features matter more for "local" than young speech.
- **Farewell to the Founders: Major Dialect Changes Along the East-West New England Border**: James N. Stanford, Thomas A. Leddy-Cecere, Kenneth P. Baclawski (2012), *American Speech*.
  - Link: [doi.org/10.1215/00031283-1668190](https://doi.org/10.1215/00031283-1668190). OA: [free copy](https://read.dukeupress.edu/american-speech/article-pdf/87/2/126/230221/ASp87.2E.2StanfordEtal.pdf). Strength 4/5.
  - **Finding:** The east-west Vermont/New England contrast reported by Kurath (1939) and Carver (1987) has weakened considerably.
  - **Graph:** Feature maps east vs west
  - **Why it matters:** Regional boundaries are fading; use broad regions.
- **Structure, Chronology, and Local Social Meaning of a Supra-Local Vowel Shift: Emergence of the Low-Back-Merger Shift in New England**: Monica Nesbitt, James N. Stanford (2021), *Language Variation and Change*.
  - Link: [doi.org/10.1017/S0954394521000168](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0954394521000168). Paywalled. Strength 4/5.
  - **Finding:** 59 White Western Massachusetts speakers aged 18-89: LBMS is emerging at the expense of NCS and traditional New England features.
  - **Graph:** TRAP lowering + LOT/THOUGHT merger by age
  - **Why it matters:** Western Mass sounds different from Boston; avoid lumping New England.
- **Bostonians /r/ Speaking: A Quantitative Look at (R) in Boston**: Patricia Irwin, Naomi Nagy (2007), *University of Pennsylvania Working Papers in Linguistics (ScholarlyCommons/UPenn repository)*.
  - Link: [repository.upenn.edu/items/4e60588f-b3ab-453e-8b13-b0ec04719ed1](https://repository.upenn.edu/items/4e60588f-b3ab-453e-8b13-b0ec04719ed1). OA: [free copy](https://repository.upenn.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/d0d4671a-a995-4978-8056-793ba331d3de/content). Strength 3/5.
  - **Finding:** Boston: (R) realised as [r] 38% of the time overall (VARBRUL input 0.35); inter-coder agreement about 90% on 400 tokens; highly educated speakers around 66%.
  - **Graph:** % r-less (~62% overall) by speaker group
  - **Why it matters:** Direct calibration for "pahk the cah".
- **Black Boston and Eastern New England English in the Late 20th Century**: Monica Nesbitt, Amalia L. Robinson, Xiao Dong (2025), *University of Pennsylvania Working Papers in Linguistics (ScholarlyCommons/UPenn repository)*.
  - Link: [repository.upenn.edu/items/7b52dd76-cce7-4f0c-84c6-d5abcf5ce15d](https://repository.upenn.edu/items/7b52dd76-cce7-4f0c-84c6-d5abcf5ce15d). OA: [free copy](https://repository.upenn.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/031c00f8-97a6-45ba-903c-120657d3ec03/content). Strength 3/5.
  - **Finding:** 23 African American and Caribbean American women born 1982-2000 in Boston: r-lessness generally below 35% and often zero; no effect of birth year (p=0.173), ethnicity (p=0.058) or style (p=0.804) on MARY-MERRY-MARRY distinction.
  - **Graph:** % r-less per speaker; MARY/MERRY/MARRY Pillai
  - **Why it matters:** Boston r-lessness differs across communities.

## Atlanta & the South (14)

**Signature features you can graph**

- /ai/ monophthongisation (PRICE glide weakening): 36% of 59 Atlanta speakers. That was 59% of Black vs 17% of white speakers.
- SVS front-vowel reversals (tense/lax) are best captured as trajectories (20-80%) or Z2-Z1, not midpoints.
- Southern vowels are long (longest durations of three dialects). Drawl tokens of 374-411 ms are triphthongised.
- Retreat: urban Raleigh moved away from all five Southern front vowels across three generations (59, 108 and 160 speakers).

- **Modeling dynamic trajectories of front vowels in the American South**: Margaret E. L. Renwick, Joseph A. Stanley (2020), *The Journal of the Acoustical Society of America*.
  - Link: [doi.org/10.1121/10.0000549](https://doi.org/10.1121/10.0000549). OA: [free copy](https://asa.scitation.org/doi/pdf/10.1121/10.0000549). Strength 5/5.
  - **Finding:** 64-speaker Digital Archive of Southern Speech: dynamic trajectories of front vowels differ by social group.
  - **Graph:** Trajectory (20-80%) of /eɪ/ and /ɪ/: Southern "drawl" movement
  - **Why it matters:** Southern-ness lives in trajectories, not midpoints.
- **Urban rejection of the vernacular: The SVS undone**: Robin Dodsworth, Mary Kohn (2012), *Language Variation and Change*.
  - Link: [doi.org/10.1017/S0954394512000105](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0954394512000105). Paywalled. Strength 5/5.
  - **Finding:** 59 white-collar Raleigh natives over three generations: all five front vowels moved away from Southern variants.
  - **Graph:** Front vowels F1/F2 by generation
  - **Why it matters:** Urban Southern youth may not sound Southern.
- **The Southern Shift in Memphis, Tennessee**: Valerie Fridland (1999), *Language Variation and Change*.
  - Link: [doi.org/10.1017/S0954394599113024](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0954394599113024). Paywalled. Strength 4/5.
  - **Finding:** Instrumental analysis of vowel systems of 25 native Memphians; describes Southern Shift movement.
  - **Graph:** Southern Shift: tense/lax front-vowel nuclei reverse (e.g. /e/ nucleus lowers toward /ɛ/); exact Hz UNVERIFIED
  - **Why it matters:** Defines SVS reversals for scoring.
- **‘Tie, tied and tight’: The expansion of /ai/ monophthongization in African‐American and European‐American speech in Memphis, Tennessee**: Valerie Fridland (2003), *Journal of Sociolinguistics*.
  - Link: [doi.org/10.1111/1467-9481.00225](https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-9481.00225). Paywalled. Strength 4/5.
  - **Finding:** /ai/ monophthongisation spreading to pre-voiceless contexts ("tight") among African Americans in Memphis. Numbers UNVERIFIED.
  - **Graph:** % monophthongal PRICE by following voicing
  - **Why it matters:** Pre-voiceless "tight" monophthong is the strongest Southern cue.
- **Variation in perception and production of mid front vowels in the U.S. Southern Vowel Shift**: Tyler Kendall, Valerie Fridland (2012), *Journal of Phonetics*.
  - Link: [doi.org/10.1016/j.wocn.2011.12.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.wocn.2011.12.002). Paywalled. Strength 4/5.
  - **Finding:** Southern production and perception of mid front vowels are linked. Numbers UNVERIFIED.
  - **Graph:** /e/ vs /ɛ/ F1/F2 by region
  - **Why it matters:** Perception-production link suggests locals hear these differences.
- **Durational and spectral differences in American English vowels: Dialect variation within and across regions**: Valerie Fridland, Tyler Kendall, Charlie Farrington (2014), *The Journal of the Acoustical Society of America*.
  - Link: [doi.org/10.1121/1.4883599](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.4883599). Paywalled. Strength 4/5.
  - **Finding:** Positive correlation between spectral overlap and vowel duration for Northern and Western speakers; durational correlates of the low-back merger and of SVS front-vowel reversals.
  - **Graph:** Vowel duration vs spectral overlap
  - **Why it matters:** Use duration as a second cue when formants overlap.
- **Regional relationships among the low vowels of U.S. English: Evidence from production and perception**: Tyler Kendall, Valerie Fridland (2017), *Language Variation and Change*.
  - Link: [doi.org/10.1017/S0954394517000084](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0954394517000084). Paywalled. Strength 4/5.
  - **Finding:** Regional relationships among low vowels. Numbers UNVERIFIED.
